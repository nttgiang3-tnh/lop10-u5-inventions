GRAMMAR

💡 Grammar — Present Perfect (have / has + V3)

📝 Form & Experience / Result

Form: have / has + past participle (V3). Use the present perfect for something that happened in the past but is still true or important NOW, or for life experiences.

• I have lost my key. (= I lost it in the past — and I can't open the door now.)

• Scientists have invented many useful devices.

• Have you ever tried an e-reader?

⏰ since / for — Started in the Past, Still True

Use since + a point in time (since 2020, since Tet) and for + a period of time (for three years, for a long time).

• Phong has saved money since Tet holiday.

• They have used this software for two years.

• Since laptops were invented, they have changed our lives.

⚡ just / already / yet / recently — for very recent or completed actions:
• He has just finished his homework. (just = a very short time ago)
• We have already seen the robot exhibition. (already = before we expected)
• Has she charged her phone yet? · She hasn't charged it yet. (yet = in questions and negatives, at the end)
⚠️ Do NOT use the present perfect with a finished past time: Edison invented the light bulb in 1879. (past simple — NOT 'has invented')

STAGE 3 · READING

📖 Reading — A Visit to the Invention Exhibition

Read the story, then answer the questions.

One Saturday morning, Mai and Tuan's class visited the City Science Museum to see a special Invention Exhibition. In the hall of famous inventions, their teacher showed them the first telephone and the light bulb invented by Thomas Edison. “Inventions like these have completely changed our lives,” she said. Next, Mai tried an e-reader. She was amazed because the small device could store hundreds of books. “I have never seen anything like it!” she said.

Then Tuan chatted with a chatbot on a big screen. It answered all his questions and booked a ticket for the science show in seconds. After that, a robot guide walked towards the class, greeted everyone and talked with the visitors. The students also watched a robot vacuum cleaner at work. “You only press the Clean button — or the Start button — and it cleans the whole hall by itself,” the teacher explained. “It is so convenient!” Mai said.

Before they left, the teacher asked: “Inventions have changed the way we live, study and work. Which invention do you like best?” The students discussed their favourite inventions happily. On the bus home, Mai smiled and said: “One day, I want to invent a useful device for our classroom.” Tuan agreed at once: “Great idea! Let's present it at the school Technology Fair!”

STAGE 3 · SPEAKING

🗣️ Speaking — Talk about inventions and their uses

Work in pairs. Talk about the uses of these inventions: a smartphone, an e-reader and a robot vacuum cleaner. Say what each invention helps us to do, and give an example from your own life.

Useful language: … help(s) / allow(s) us to… · We can use … to / for… · … is used for… · For example, … · I use it every day to… · It is very convenient because…

A: Let's talk about inventions. In my opinion, the smartphone is the most useful one. Smartphones help us to study, communicate and find information quickly. We can use them to learn English with educational apps.
B: I agree. I also love e-readers. An e-reader is used for storing and reading hundreds of books, so I can read novels anywhere.
A: And what about robot vacuum cleaners?
B: They allow us to clean the house without doing any work. My family uses one every weekend — it is very convenient!

STAGE 3 · WRITING

✍️ Writing — The benefits of smartphones in learning

Write a paragraph (100–120 words) about the benefits of smartphones in learning. Give TWO or THREE benefits with examples, and use at least FIVE words from this lesson.

Sentence starters: Smartphones are one of the most useful inventions for students. · Firstly, smartphones help us to… · For example, … · Secondly, they allow us to… · Finally, … · In short, …

Useful language: smartphone · device · convenient · valuable · software / apps · store information · communicate · look up new words · study anywhere · suitable for learning

Smartphones are one of the most useful inventions for students today. Firstly, these devices help us to learn anywhere and at any time. For example, we can use educational apps to practise vocabulary or watch lessons again at home. Secondly, smartphones allow us to look up new words and store a lot of information, photos and documents in a small device. They are also convenient for communicating with teachers and classmates when we work in groups. In short, smartphones are a valuable tool that makes learning easier, faster and more interesting for every student.
